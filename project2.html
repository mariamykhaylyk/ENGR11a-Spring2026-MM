CaliCat

The CaliCat is a cute project to test your 3D printer! I learned about torture-tests and calibration tests in class, and chose the CaliCat design to print and analyze. I learned about bridging, layer separation and splitting, blobs and zits, gaps, poor surfaces above supports, and more.

Additionally, I used an STL file to print my CaliCat because I learned STL files are simple and work with almost every 3D printer and slicer. STL files only store the shape of the model, while 3MF files can include extra information like scale, colors, and print settings. Understanding this helped me realize why STL is so common, but also why 3MF can be more useful for saving complete print projects.

Here are my detailed notes on the CaliCat:

a. I scaled down the print slightly to make it finish faster, and the dimensions were accurate.

b. When I was introduced to the concept of a torture test, I thought it meant the design wouldn’t print. I chose the CaliCat because I wanted to see what could go wrong with it, but it did print pretty well and was more of calibration test.

c. I saw barely any stringing, only slightly between the ears (shows good retraction).

d. Tail is overall great, just a little spot that has an indent due to overhang (good overhang performance).

e. The underside of the cat’s body is bumpy above the feet (ok bridging) and is slightly disconnected from the back feet (layer splitting).

f. Finally, the cat is not perfectly smooth (gaps and zits), but overall looks great.
