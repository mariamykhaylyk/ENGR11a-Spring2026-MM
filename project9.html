Snazzy Shoez: Design

Our problem statement for Snazzy Shoez is “Individuals with only one functional hand lack a practical way to tie standard shoelaces because existing solutions fail to provide full autonomy and are too conspicuous for formal wear.”

We have modified our project statement into a need statement: Individuals with only one functional hand NEED a practical way to tie standard shoelaces to increase autonomy.

For our design to satisfy this need, we have a few core principles. Our tool should be easy to put in your shoe with one hand, it should aid in grip and tension during the process, and it should be strong enough to not snap under the force of shoe tying. Our quantitative measure of success for the design will be how much force the tool can take before it breaks.

Our first design looks similar to a screw. You will insert the tool into one of the holes in your shoe. The large base at the bottom ensures that the peg can go through any hole and stay there, regardless of the size of the hole. We were thinking of potentially adding a keychain handle which allows you to use the tool in one of two ways; either you can leave it in your shoe permanently, or take it out and carry it with you on a keychain throughout the day. The user can choose what works best for them. We plan to make the design with and without the keychain in testing. The threaded part of the stem will help hold the shoelaces in place because it adds some texture. If it was too smooth, the shoelaces may slip off during the tying process.

~Cycle 1: First Prototype~

We designed our first prototype on OnShape. The base diameter is 8mm and extruded 2mm. This should be large enough to extend beyond the edges of any hole in a shoe. The stem is 3mm in diameter and 20mm extruded. We added the screw-like piece with the custom added feature “Plastic Screw”. We printed in PETG at .20mm speed with a brim.

To test it, we did a stress test to see how much force we could place on the item before it snapped. Results: 7N, snapped near the base. We determined that you need at least 11N for shoe tying. The stem looked very messy; it was bendy and unstable.

~Cycle 2: CAD Change~

For our second prototype, we added the keychain feature to the base. We also used a different method to create the screw piece by watching a video tutorial. We wanted to see if a different structure would be stronger and cleaner. It had fewer rotations and was slightly thicker. We printed the design with the same settings. The new design made the print much more aesthetically pleasing! The stress test for this shape was more successful at 14N!

~Cycle 3: CAM Change~

For our third iteration, we decided to change the speed of the print. For the first two, the tool printed in under five minutes. We wondered if it was printed slower, would the adhesion be stronger and would the shape therefore be more sturdy and straight through layer consolidation? Using the design from iteration 2, we changed the speed to .10mm from .20mm. The new stress test was very successful! The force gauge showed 26N before snapping, well above the 11N needed for shoe tying. The print itself appeared slightly thicker than the faster version; this is because the slower speed allowed for more detail so the screw seemed more precise and clean.

~Cycle 4: Material Change~

For our fourth prototype, we tested out a new material. We used the shape from design 2 but printed it with PLA. The stress test actually showed 24N which was higher than we anticipated. This showed us that for a print of this size, a sturdy design shape is more important than the material.

~Reflection~

We will keep our strongest prototype from cycle 3. The CAD change made the biggest difference in our process – a simple change in the design shape doubled the force that the tool could withstand from 7 N to 14 N. We learned the most from the CAM change because slowing down the print speed took our previous prototype from a 14 N stress test result to 26 N. The speed of the print has a greater impact than we expected.

Our next steps are to finalize our user guide for Snazzy Shoez based on our original storyboard. We will test how well our instructions work and determine packaging and marketing materials.
